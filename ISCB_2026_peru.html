AI-assisted Curation and the                  expansion 

Pfam  is  a  publicly  available,  comprehensive  repository  of  protein  families.  It  is  represented  by  manually  curated  multiple  sequence  alignments  and  hidden  Markov 

models and is a key resource for biological research. In recent years, Pfam has been fully integrated into InterPro. This  enabled streamlined management and more 

frequent updates. This transition resulted in an increase of 809 families, from 24,736 to 25,545. More recently, the introduction of AI-assisted curation has further 

accelerated Pfam expansion. Here, we describe the AI-assisted curation workflow developed to support this expansion and the data to show the Pfam increase.

The workflow: combing annotation, structural data and visibility

Which have been the two major changes in Pfam?

Until  release  37.0,  Pfam  was  updated  yearly,  adding 

~800  families  per  release.  Pfam's  growth  has  since 

been  driven  by  two  major  changes.  First,  integration 

into  InterPro  brought  two-month  releases  from  Pfam 

37.1  onwards.  Together  with  AlphaFold2  models  and 

the collaboration with ECOD, this added 3,605 families 

between  Pfam  37.1  and  38.0,  almost  as  many  as  the 

previous  ﬁve  yearly  releases  combined.  Secondly, 

AI-assisted  curation  added  1,936  families  in  Pfam 

38.1  and  2,653  in  38.2,  mainly  by  bringing  all  the 

evidence a curator needs into a single tool and by using 

LLM-drafted  annotations  to  accelerate  the  process. 

Automatic  annotation  of  uncharacterised  families 

(auto-DUFs)  further  expanded  this  growth,  adding 

23,139  families  in  Pfam  39.0  alone  and  reaching 

53,273 families and 1,050 clans.

TED domain distribution and Pfam architecture

Biological context

Candidate domains from TED are refined into Pfam families through 
iterative HMM searches. An interactive pipeline then brings all the 
evidence a curator needs into a single view (including SCOOP, 
HHsearch, Dali, Foldseek, Paperblast, String, disulphide oligomeric), 
and an LLM drafts the annotation for curator review.
